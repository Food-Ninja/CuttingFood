Here's a grouping of the actions based on their parameterization in the context of cutting fruits and vegetables:

**Group 1:  Dicing/Cubing**

* **Representative:** Dice
* **Members:** Cube, Chop (can overlap with other groups depending on size)
* **Parameters:**
    * **Cutting position:**  Various, often across the entire object
    * **Amount of repetitions:** Multiple, aiming for uniform pieces
    * **Input object/shape:** Whole or roughly shaped fruit/vegetable
    * **Output object/shape:** Small, roughly cubic pieces

**Group 2: Slicing**

* **Representative:** Slice
* **Members:**  Halve, Quarter, Sliver
* **Parameters:**
    * **Cutting position:**  Along a single axis, creating flat pieces
    * **Amount of repetitions:**  One or multiple, depending on desired thickness
    * **Input object/shape:** Whole or roughly shaped fruit/vegetable
    * **Output object/shape:** Flat pieces of varying thickness

**Group 3:  Fine Cutting**

* **Representative:** Mince
* **Members:** Julienne, Snip
* **Parameters:**
    * **Cutting position:**  Very small, precise cuts
    * **Amount of repetitions:** Many
    * **Input object/shape:** Often pre-sliced or chopped
    * **Output object/shape:** Very small, uniform pieces

**Excluded Actions:**

* **Divide:** Too general. While it could apply, it lacks the specificity of the other actions.
* **Saw:**  Implies a sawing motion, not typical for fruit and vegetable preparation.
* **Carve:** Usually implies shaping or decorative cutting, not standard preparation.
* **Pare:**  Specifically refers to removing the skin, a distinct action.
* **Cut:** Too general. It encompasses all the other actions. 


**Important Notes:**

* **Overlap:** Some actions like "chop" can fall into multiple groups depending on the size and style of the cut.
* **Context:** The specific parameters can vary based on the type of fruit or vegetable and the desired outcome.
